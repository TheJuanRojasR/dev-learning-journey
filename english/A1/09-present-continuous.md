# Present Continuous

> The present continuous describes actions happening now, temporary situations, and arranged future plans.

## Form

| Subject | Auxiliary | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| I | `am` | `working` |
| he / she / it | `is` | `working` |
| you / we / they | `are` | `working` |

## `-ing` Spelling

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| most verbs: `+ing` | `work → working` |
| ends in silent `e`: drop the `e` | `write → writing`, `make → making` |
| 1 syllable CVC: double the consonant | `run → running`, `sit → sitting`, `swim → swimming` |
| ends in `ie`: `ie → y` | `lie → lying`, `die → dying` |

## Uses

1. Now: `I am studying right now.`
2. Temporary: `She is living in Bogotá this month.`
3. Future arrangements: `I'm meeting my boss tomorrow.`
4. Changes: `The world is changing.`

## Time markers

`now`, `right now`, `at the moment`, `currently`, `today`, `this week / month`

## Negative / Questions / Short answers

- Negative: `I'm not working.` / `He isn't working.` / `They aren't working.`
- Question: `Am I working?` / `Is he working?` / `Are they working?`
- Short answers: `Yes, I am.` / `No, he isn't.`

## Present Simple vs Present Continuous

| Present simple (habit) | Present continuous (now) |
|---|---|
| `I work every day.` | `I am working right now.` |
| `She usually walks to work.` | `Today she is taking the bus.` |

## Stative Verbs (never in continuous)

`like`, `love`, `hate`, `want`, `need`, `know`, `understand`, `believe`, `remember`, `have` (possess)

- ✅ `I like this song.` ❌ `I'm liking this song.`
- ✅ `He knows the answer.` ❌ `He's knowing the answer.`

## Common Mistakes (from our lessons)

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `I'm like this song.` | `I like this song.` | stative → simple |
| `He's know the answer.` | `He knows the answer.` | stative + invalid `is` + base |
| `I'm wanting a coffee.` | `I want a coffee.` | stative |
| `He's needing help.` | `He needs help.` | stative |
| `She never eat meat.` | `She never eats meat.` | third-person `-s` |

## Tips

1. Form: `am/is/are` + `-ing` — never forget the auxiliary.
2. Stative verbs → always simple, even for "right now".
3. Spelling: `e` drops, CVC doubles, `ie → y`.
4. Habit = simple; now = continuous.
5. In simple sentences, the third-person `-s` still applies.
