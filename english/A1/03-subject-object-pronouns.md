# Subject & Object Pronouns

> Pronouns replace nouns. Subject pronouns do the action; object pronouns receive it or follow prepositions.

## Main Rules

| Role | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Subject** (before verb) | I, you, he, she, it | we, you, they |
| **Object** (after verb / preposition) | me, you, him, her, it | us, you, them |

## Position

| Type | Position | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| Subject pronoun | Before verb | `He reads.` |
| Object pronoun | After verb | `I know him.` |
| Object pronoun | After preposition | `for me`, `with her`, `to them` |

## Key Points

- English **never omits the subject** (Spanish: *voy* → English: `I go`).
- After **any preposition** (`for`, `with`, `to`, `between`, `at`...) use **object pronoun**: `for me`, `between you and me`.
- `they / we / you / I` + verb in present → **no `-s`**: `They see`, not `They sees`.
- Formal comparison: `than I / than he / than she` (not `than me / him / her`).
- `talk **to** someone` (not `talk someone`).

## Common Mistakes (from practice)

| Error | Correction | Reason |
|-------|------------|--------|
| `Between you and I` | `Between you and me` | After preposition → object pronoun |
| `They sees her` | `They see her` | `they` = plural → verb without `-s` |
| `She is taller than me` | `She is taller than I` | Formal: `than` + subject pronoun |
| `talk her` | `talk to her` | Verb `talk` requires `to` |
| `a developers` | `a developer` / `developers` | Article + singular OR plural without `a` |
| `same me` | `same as me` / `like me` | Collocation: `same as` / `like` |
