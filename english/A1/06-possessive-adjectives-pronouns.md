# Possessive Adjectives & Pronouns

> Possessives show who something belongs to. English has three forms: an adjective (before a noun), a pronoun (alone), and an object pronoun (receiver / after a preposition).

## The Three Functions

| Owner | Adjective (+ noun) | Pronoun (alone) | Object (action / after prep.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | `my` | `mine` | `me` |
| you | `your` | `yours` | `you` |
| he | `his` | `his` | `him` |
| she | `her` | `hers` | `her` |
| it | `its` | — | `it` |
| we | `our` | `ours` | `us` |
| they | `their` | `theirs` | `them` |

## The 2-Question Test

1. Is there a **noun after the gap**? → possessive **adjective**  
   `___ car` → `my car`
2. Does the gap **end the clause**? → possessive **pronoun**  
   `It's ___` → `It's mine.`
3. Does it **receive the action / follow a preposition**? → **object pronoun**  
   `for ___` → `for us` / `with him`

## Examples

- `My laptop is old, but mine still works.`
- `Their house is big, but ours is bigger.`
- `This gift is for her.`
- `Between us and them, there is a river.`
- `Those mistakes are theirs.`

## Common Mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `ours keys` | `our keys` | a pronoun never goes before a noun |
| `her's car` | `hers` / `her car` | possessive pronouns take **no** apostrophe |
| `The car is them.` | `The car is theirs.` | after `be`, use the possessive pronoun |
| `The victory was us.` | `The victory was ours.` | same rule |
| `Theirs have a good mood.` | `They are in a good mood.` | subject needs a subject pronoun |
| `thanked theirs` | `thanked me / us` | object needs an object pronoun |
| `between our and them` | `between us and them` | after a preposition → object pronoun |
| `bigger than our` | `bigger than ours` | clause-end → pronoun |

## Key Rule

> Never put a possessive pronoun before a noun.  
> ❌ `ours keys` → ✅ `our keys`

## Quick Review

1. Noun after the gap? → **adjective** (`my`, `your`, `their`...).
2. Gap ends the sentence? → **pronoun** (`mine`, `yours`, `theirs`...).
3. Receives the action / after a preposition? → **object pronoun** (`me`, `us`, `them`...).
4. Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe (`hers`, not `her's`).
5. `its` is only an adjective; there is no possessive pronoun for `it`.
