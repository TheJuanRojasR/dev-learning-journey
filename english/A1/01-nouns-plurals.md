# Nouns and Plurals

> Nouns can be singular or plural. Plural nouns usually refer to more than one person, animal, place, or thing.

## Main Rules

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Add `-s` to most nouns | `book → books` |
| Add `-es` after `s`, `sh`, `ch`, `x`, or `z` | `box → boxes` |
| Change consonant + `y` to `-ies` | `city → cities` |
| Some `-f/-fe` nouns change to `-ves` | `leaf → leaves` |
| Some plurals are irregular | `child → children` |

## Special Plurals

- Same form: `one sheep → two sheep`
- Same form: `one deer → two deer`
- Singular word ending in `-s`: `The news is good.`
- Plural-only nouns: `These trousers are new.`
- `Police` takes a plural verb: `The police are outside.`

## Collective Nouns

Collective nouns name a group.

- `The family is happy. It is united.`
- `The class is quiet.`
- `Two families are visiting.`

## Common Mistakes

- `two sheeps` → `two sheep`
- `The news are good.` → `The news is good.`
