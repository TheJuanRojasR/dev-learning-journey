# Verb to Be

> The verb "to be" means *ser* or *estar*. It changes with the subject and is irregular.

## Main Rules

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| I | am |
| He / She / It | is |
| You / We / They | are |

## Forms

- Affirmative: `I am a student.`
- Negative: `He is not here.` → `He isn't here.`
- Question: `She is happy.` → `Is she happy?`
- Negative question: `We are ready.` → `Aren't we ready?`
- Short answer: `Yes, I am.` / `No, he isn't.`

## Contractions

| Full | Contracted |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| He is | He's |
| They are | They're |
| Is not | isn't |
| Are not | aren't |

## Important Notes

- Contractions are **informal**. In formal writing (like emails), use the full form: `I am`, not `I'm`.
- Negative questions **always** use a contraction: `Isn't she happy?`, not `Is not she happy?`
  - `Aren't I late?` is the natural form.

## Common Mistakes

- `I not is a student.` → `I am not a student.`
- `Is not she from here?` → `Isn't she from here?`
- `Why she isn't here?` → `Why isn't she here?` (don't change the meaning)
