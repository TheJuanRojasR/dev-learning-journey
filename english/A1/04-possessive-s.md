# Possessive 's

> The possessive 's shows possession: *John's car* = the car of John.

## Main Rules

| Possessor | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | add `'s` | `John's car` |
| Plural regular (-s) | add `'` only | `the students' books` |
| Plural irregular | add `'s` | `the children's toys` |
| Name ending in -s | `'s` or `'` | `James's` / `James'` |

## Key Contrast: Shared vs Separate

- **Shared** (one thing): `John and Mary's car` (one `'s`)
- **Separate** (each has one): `John's and Mary's cars` (two `'s` + plural)

## Possessive with Gerunds

The owner of the action takes `'s`:

- `I don't like Peter's coming late.` (I don't like that Peter arrives late.)
- `The teacher doesn't accept the students' cheating.`

## Double Possessive (no apostrophe)

After `of`, use possessive pronouns, not object pronouns:

| Object | Possessive pronoun |
|---|---|
| me | mine |
| you | yours |
| her | hers |
| him | his |
| us | ours |
| them | theirs |

- `a friend of mine`
- `that idea of hers`
- `some friends of yours`

## Possessive at the end of a sentence

The owned noun can be left out:

- `That house is the neighbors'.`
- `Whose car is this? It's my brother's.`

## Common Mistakes

- `the men'` → `the men's` (irregular plural needs `'s`)
- `that idea of her` → `that idea of hers`
- `the John and Mary's car` → `John and Mary's car` (no article before names)
